Welcome to México — and to color
This week: the colors, and how to say where things are on a map.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Mexico is a country of color — green, white, and red on its flag; bright markets; painted buildings. This week you learn to name those colors and say where places are.
We're starting a two-week trip through Mexico. This module gives you two tools: the colors (los colores), and a few words for direction and location so you can talk about a map. Best of all, some colors you learn will unlock the meaning of US place names you see every day.

📚 PART 1 · WARM UP — THE PATTERN YOU OWN

Back in Module 2 you learned that a word's ending often tells you its gender: -o usually means el (masculine), and -a usually means la (feminine). Hold onto that — it comes back this week in a new way, with colors.

🔍 PART 2 · A PEEK AT LOS COLORES

Here are three colors to start. Say each one out loud — two of them are close cousins of English words, so you almost know them already:

rojo (red)
azul (blue)
verde (green)

Do you see it? Verde is a cousin of "verdant" (leafy green), and it hides in names like Palo Verde. Tomorrow you'll meet the full set of colors with sound — and one of them, amarillo ("yellow"), is also the name of a city in Texas!

💡 The question to hold this week: if colors are describing words, do they change to match the noun — the way amigo became amiga? Keep that in mind. In Lesson 8.03 it gets a clear answer.
