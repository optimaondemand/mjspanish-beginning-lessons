Lesson 8.04 · Spanish in Your World
On the map — and the colors hiding in it
Say where a place is, then discover the Spanish colors inside US place names.
🌍 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
A few small words let you point to any spot on a map — and the colors you just learned are quietly living inside American place names.

First you'll learn to say north, south, east, west and whether a place is near or far. Then comes the fun part: Colorado and Amarillo are colors you now know — hiding in plain sight on the US map.

🧭 PART 1 · LOS PUNTOS CARDINALES (THE DIRECTIONS)

These four direction words are all masculine (el). Read the compass out loud:

el norte
north
el oeste
west
México
el este
east
el sur
south
🔊 PART 2 · SAYING WHERE A PLACE IS

To say where something is, Spanish uses a ready-made little word: está ("it is / it is located"). For now, just learn it as a set phrase — the full verb behind it (and its partner) comes later in the year. Press ▶ and repeat:

está en…

it is in… (a place)

al norte de…

to the north of…

cerca de…

near / close to…

lejos de…

far from…

Put it together on the map of Mexico:
Cancún está en el este. (Cancún is in the east.)
Tijuana está al norte, cerca de los Estados Unidos. (Tijuana is in the north, near the United States.)

💡 You're using está as a fixed phrase for now. Later this year you'll meet the full verb it comes from — and its partner verb — together, so the whole system clicks at once.

✅ APPLY IT
Use your directions and your new map eyes.
You want to say a city is close to the ocean. Which phrase do you use?
The state name COLORADO is a cousin of which Spanish color?
"Cancún está en el este." What does this tell you?
★ WHY THIS MATTERS

A huge stretch of the United States — from Colorado to California to Florida — was named by Spanish speakers. Every time you learn a color, you can suddenly read a place name that thousands of people pass every day without knowing what it means. Now you do.
