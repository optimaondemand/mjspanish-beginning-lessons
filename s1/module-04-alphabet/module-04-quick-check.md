## Items

### Q1 — The big idea (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Module 4 said Spanish is **phonetic**. What does that mean?
- **A) Its spelling matches its sound — you say what you see** ✅
- B) It has no vowels
- C) Every letter is silent
- D) It changes sound from town to town

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! Phonetic = spelling matches sound. That is why you can read a new Spanish word out loud."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Phonetic means the spelling matches the sound — you say what you see. That is Spanish's gift to a reader."

---

### Q2 — The alphabet (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** How many letters are in the Spanish alphabet?
- A) 26 (the same as English)
- **B) 27 — the English letters plus ñ** ✅
- C) 30

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Correcto! 27 — the 26 English letters plus **ñ**, which is its own letter."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "It is **27**: the same 26 as English, plus **ñ** (eñe)."

---

### Q3 — Silent h (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** In the word **hola**, what happens to the letter **h**?
- **A) It is silent — you say "OH-la"** ✅
- B) It sounds like English "h"
- C) It sounds like a rolling r

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Muy bien! The Spanish **h** is silent, so *hola* = 'OH-la'."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "The Spanish **h** makes no sound — *hola* sounds like 'OH-la'."

---

### Q4 — r vs. rr (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Which word has the **rolling rr** sound?
- A) pero (but)
- **B) perro (dog)** ✅

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! *perro* (dog) has the rolled **rr**. *pero* (but) is a single soft tap — the roll changes the meaning!"
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*perro* (dog) is the rolled one. *pero* (but) is a soft single tap. One little roll changes the word."

---

### Q5 — Letter name (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** What is the Spanish name of the letter **ñ**?
- A) ene
- **B) eñe** ✅
- C) ese

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Exacto! **ñ** is called *eñe*. (*ene* is plain n; *ese* is s.)"
*Feedback (incorrect):* "**ñ** is *eñe*. (*ene* is n, *ese* is s.)"

---

### Q6 — Letter name recognition (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Your teacher says the letter name **«jota»**. Which letter is it?
- A) G
- **B) J** ✅
- C) Y

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Correcto! *jota* = the letter **J**."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*jota* is the name of the letter **J**. (G = *ge*, Y = *ye*.)"

---

### Q7 — Reading a place name (Multiple Choice) — *light, not a graded checkpoint*
**Prompt:** **Los Ángeles** is a Spanish place name. Using **Los** (one of the four words for "the"), it means…
- **A) "the angels"** ✅
- B) "the beaches"
- C) "the lights"

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! *Los Ángeles* = 'the angels' — and the mark on the Á is the *acento*."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*Los Ángeles* means 'the angels.' *Los* = 'the' (plural), *Ángeles* = 'angels.'"

## Alignment notes (for the builder / reviewer)

- **7 items, gate at 70% (5/7), unlimited attempts, highest score kept** — matches the brief's gentle-gate policy (§3) and the "ambitious but relaxed" principle.
- Coverage map: Q1 phonetic principle · Q2 alphabet size · Q3 silent h · Q4 r/rr contrast · Q5–Q6 letter names · Q7 light place-name reading.
- **New content is tested on known vocabulary.** The phonetic examples (hola, pero/perro) and letter names are M04's own material; no item asks students to spell an unknown word from memory. This honors "new grammar/orthography on known vocab; never both at once."
- Correct-answer position is varied (A, B, A, B, B, B, A); two-option items force A/B and shuffle is ON.
- This is **not** an S1 graded culture-link checkpoint. Q7 is a single light reading item and is intentionally kept to *reading* the name; the **meaning** assessment of place names is M05's job (place-name-meaning checkpoint), so there is no overlap.
