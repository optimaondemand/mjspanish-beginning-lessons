📚 PART 1 · A CODE YOU CAN ACTUALLY CRACK

English spelling is famously tricky. Think of though, through, tough, and thought — same letters, wildly different sounds. English readers just memorize thousands of words one at a time.

Spanish plays fair. Almost every letter makes one reliable sound, so once you know the alphabet you can look at a brand-new word and read it correctly. That's the payoff of this week — not a pile of facts, but a key that unlocks the reading of the whole language.

💡 PART 3 · ONE WORD FOR THE WEEK: "PHONETIC"

A language is phonetic when its spelling matches its sound — you say what you see. Spanish is one of the most phonetic languages in the world. English… is not, and every English reader knows it.

That's the whole reason to learn the alphabet properly now: it isn't busywork, it's the tool that lets you read every word in every future lesson. Tomorrow we look at exactly how the "one letter, one sound" idea works — and where English trips over it.
