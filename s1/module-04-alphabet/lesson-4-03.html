💡 Good to know: these are the official pan-Hispanic letter names. Across much of Latin America you'll also hear be called "be grande / be larga," uve called "ve chica / ve corta," and ye called "i griega." All are correct — they name the same letters.
🔥 PART 2 · THE SOFT r AND THE ROLLING rr

Spanish has two R sounds, and they can change a word's meaning:

  • A single r between vowels is a soft, quick tap — close to the "dd" in the American "ladder."
  • A double rr (or an r at the start of a word) is rolled — a trilled "rrr."

Listen to each pair and try to match it. The rolling sound takes practice — that is completely normal.

pero
but — soft, single r
perro
dog — rolled rr
caro
expensive — soft, single r
carro
car — rolled rr
rojo
red — r at the start rolls
rápido
fast — r at the start rolls
💬 Hear the difference: pero (but) and perro (dog) are the same except for the roll — one soft tap vs. a trill. If the roll won't come yet, keep tapping the single r; it clicks with practice.
