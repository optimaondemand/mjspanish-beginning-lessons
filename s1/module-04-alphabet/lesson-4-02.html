LESSON 4.02 · THE BIG IDEA OF THE MODULE
Spanish is spelled the way it sounds
Understand this one thing and the whole alphabet makes sense.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
In Spanish, a letter almost always makes the same sound — so spelling and pronunciation line up. This is what "phonetic" means, and it is Spanish's gift to you.
We'll use only words you already know from Modules 1–3, so you can focus on the idea — not on new vocabulary. You'll see where English breaks the rule, watch Spanish keep it, and try predicting a sound yourself.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
explain what "phonetic" means, and why it makes Spanish easier to read than English.
2
predict how a Spanish word sounds just by reading its letters.
📚 PART 1 · WHERE ENGLISH TRIPS

In English, the same letters can make totally different sounds. Say these four out loud — the "ough" changes every time:

though  ·  through  ·  tough  ·  thought

Nothing on the page tells you which sound to use — you just memorized each word. Spanish refuses to do this to you.

🇬🇧 English spelling: UNPREDICTABLE

One spelling, many sounds. You memorize each word's pronunciation separately.

🇪🇸 Spanish spelling: PREDICTABLE

Each letter has a steady sound. Read the letters and you have the word — even a new one.

📚 PART 2 · SPANISH KEEPS ITS WORD

Take words you already know. Every letter says exactly what it should, and every vowel gets its clean sound. Read each one slowly, letter by letter:

taco
TA-ko — a=ah, o=oh. No surprises.
mesa
ME-sa — e=eh, a=ah. Exactly as spelled.
libro
LEE-bro — i=ee, o=oh. Say every letter.
fiesta
fee-ES-ta — five letters, five clean sounds.

One small heads-up for tomorrow: a few Spanish letters have a quirk worth knowing — the h is silent (that is why hola sounds like "OH-la"), and r at the start of a word rolls. But even these are rules, not random — once you know them, they never change on you.

✅ PART 3 · PREDICT THE SOUND

Use the "one letter, one sound" idea. No memorizing — just apply it.

🎯 READ IT RIGHT
Using the Spanish vowels, how does each word sound?
The word silla (chair) — how does the i sound?
How many vowels do you pronounce in mesa?
In hola, what happens to the h?
