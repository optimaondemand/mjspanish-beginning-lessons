Why US places sound Spanish

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Hundreds of US cities and states have Spanish names — and now you can actually read them, letter by letter, the way they were meant to sound.
Today you put your alphabet to work on real place names: Los Ángeles, San Diego, Santa Fe. You'll notice how the Spanish letters explain the way these names have always sounded. (Next module, M5, digs into what the names mean — today is about reading them.)
🌎 Spanish in Your World

The map was written in Spanish first

Spanish explorers and settlers named much of the American West long before English speakers arrived — so those names follow Spanish spelling and Spanish sounds. When you say them "the American way," you're actually saying slightly worn-down Spanish. Read them with your new alphabet and they snap into focus:

Los Ángeles — "the angels"
San Diego — "Saint Diego"
Santa Fe — "holy faith"

Notice the pieces you can already read: San and Santa mean "saint / holy," Los is one of your four words for "the," and the accent on Ángeles is the acento you met yesterday.
