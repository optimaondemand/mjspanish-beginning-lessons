## Items

### Q1 — SER + adjective (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Which verb do you use to say what a person is *like* (their personality)?
- **A) *ser* (soy, eres, es, somos, son)** ✅
- B) a brand-new verb introduced this week
- C) you don't need a verb at all
- D) *gustar*

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! You already owned **ser** from Module 13 — this week you just added the describing words."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "It's **ser** — the same 'to be' verb you learned in Module 13. *Yo soy… / Ella es…*"

---

### Q2 — Agreement, -o → -a (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** You want to say that a girl named Ana is generous. Which is correct?
- A) Ana es generoso.
- **B) Ana es generosa.** ✅
- C) Ana es generose.

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Muy bien! For a girl, an **-o** adjective flips to **-a**: *generosa*."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Remember the rule: describing a girl, **-o → -a**. So it's *Ana es **generosa***."

---

### Q3 — Agreement, invariable -e (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** The adjective *inteligente* ends in **-e**. To describe a girl, you…
- **A) leave it exactly the same: *ella es inteligente*** ✅
- B) change it to *inteligenta*
- C) add an *-o*: *inteligento*

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Correcto! Adjectives ending in **-e** don't change — same for a boy or a girl."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Adjectives ending in **-e** (like *inteligente*, *amable*, *valiente*) stay the same for everyone."

---

### ★ Q4 — CULTURE-LINK cognate (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** The Spanish word *inteligente* is a cognate of which English word?
- **A) intelligent** ✅
- B) interesting
- C) independent

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! Both come from Latin *intelligere*, 'to understand.'"
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*inteligente* = **intelligent** — same Latin root, tiny spelling change."

---

### ★ Q5 — CULTURE-LINK cognate (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** A Spanish report card calls a student *responsable*. In English, that student is…
- **A) responsible** ✅
- B) reasonable
- C) respectable

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Exacto! The **-able / -ble** ending is a big cognate signal: *responsable* = responsible."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*responsable* = **responsible** — watch for that -ble ending, it's a reliable cognate clue."

---

### ★ Q6 — CULTURE-LINK cognate (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** A brave hero is described as *valiente*. What is the English cognate?
- **A) valiant (brave)** ✅
- B) violent
- C) valuable

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! Both trace back to Latin *valere*, 'to be strong.' *valiente* = valiant = brave."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*valiente* = **valiant** (brave), from Latin *valere*. Not 'violent' or 'valuable.'"

---

### ★ Q7 — CULTURE-LINK cognate, FALSE FRIEND (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Careful — your host in Mexico is described as *simpática*. What does that actually mean?
- **A) nice and friendly** ✅
- B) sympathetic (feels sorry for you)
- C) simple / plain

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Muy bien! *simpático/a* is a **false friend** — it looks like 'sympathetic' but means **nice / friendly**."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Trap alert! *simpático/a* does NOT mean 'sympathetic.' It means **nice / friendly**."

---

### ★ Q8 — CULTURE-LINK cognate (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** A shy student is *tímido*. Which English word does it match?
- **A) timid** ✅
- B) tiny
- C) tired

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! *tímido* = **timid**, from Latin *timidus*, 'fearful.' Shy and timid are the same idea."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*tímido* = **timid** (shy) — same Latin root as the English word."
