LESSON 9.04 · SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD
The map of the US is half in Spanish
The same words you just learned name mountains, rivers, and whole states north of the border.
🌍 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
Spanish speakers explored and named huge stretches of what is now the United States — so their words are still sitting on your map today.

For about 300 years, much of today's American Southwest and West was part of the Spanish-speaking world — first New Spain, later Mexico. The explorers and settlers named the land in Spanish, and those names stuck. Say these out loud — every one is Spanish:

ColoradoNevadaMontanaCaliforniaEl PasoSanta FeRío Grande

You already met Colorado and Nevada back in Module 1. Today, meet a few more — and notice they use the exact geography words you learned yesterday.

Most of these names describe the land literally. Which one is the odd one out — it came from a novel?
You learned la montaña = the mountain. Which US state's name comes straight from it?
★ WHY THIS MATTERS

Mexico and the United States share more than a border — they share names, history, and family ties. Millions of people move between the two, and the Spanish on your map is a reminder that this language has been part of American life for centuries. Learning it connects you to your own country's story.
