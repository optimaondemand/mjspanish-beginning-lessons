Lesson 9.02 · The new grammar
Saying where a place sits: está al… de
One tidy pattern lets you describe any place on the map, relative to another.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday: está + a direction. Today we add one small word — de ("of / from") — and suddenly you can compare two places.
This is new grammar on words you already know. The directions are old friends from Module 8; the only new move is snapping them into the pattern X está al [direction] de Y.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
build the sentence X está al [norte/sur/este/oeste] de Y.
2
describe where one place sits compared to another, in Spanish.
📚 PART 1 · THE PATTERN

Here is the whole thing in one line. Read it slowly:

a place → está + al norte / sur / este / oeste + de + another place

In English that's just: "____ is to the [north/south/east/west] of ____." The word de is the little hinge that means "of." Watch it work:

Monterrey está al norte de la Ciudad de México.
Monterrey is to the north of Mexico City.
Guadalajara está al oeste de la Ciudad de México.
Guadalajara is to the west of Mexico City.
Oaxaca está al sur de la Ciudad de México.
Oaxaca is to the south of Mexico City.
💡 One heads-up: in Spanish, a + el squeezes together into al. That's why it's al norte, not "a el norte." You don't have to explain it — just say al and you're right.
✅ PART 2 · BUILD THE SENTENCE

Use the map facts given in each question. Pick the phrase that completes the sentence.

🎯 APPLY THE PATTERN
Everything you need is in the prompt — just choose the right direction word.
Monterrey sits north of Mexico City. Complete: "Monterrey está ___ de la Ciudad de México."
Which word is the "hinge" that means "of" in está al norte ___ México?

💪 ONE IDEA TO CARRY OUT
