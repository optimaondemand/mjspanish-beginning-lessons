LESSON 12.04 · SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD
Birthdays you've already been to
The piñata at a party, the "sweet fifteen" you've heard of — that's Spanish, right in your neighborhood.
🌍 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
Birthday traditions from Spanish-speaking cultures are woven right into life in the United States.

You may have swung at a piñata, or heard of a girl's big quinceañera. These aren't distant customs — they're part of how millions of families in the U.S. celebrate. And one of them connects straight back to the numbers you learned this week.

✨ THREE BIRTHDAY TRADITIONS
🪥 la piñata
A decorated container filled with candy and small treats, hung up at a party. Blindfolded, kids take turns swinging a stick until it breaks and the goodies spill out. It's a birthday favorite across Mexico and much of Latin America — and English borrowed the word whole, keeping the ñ.
👑 la quinceañera
In many Latin American cultures, a girl's 15th birthday is a huge celebration marking her step from childhood toward young womanhood — special dress, family, food, and dancing. Look closely at the word: quince (15) is hiding right inside it! You just learned that number.
🎵 Feliz cumpleaños a ti
When it's time to sing, many Spanish-speaking families use Feliz cumpleaños a ti — the very same tune as the English "Happy Birthday to You," just with Spanish words. You already know the phrase from yesterday: ¡Feliz cumpleaños!
★ WHY THIS MATTERS

A birthday is one of the most human things there is — and how a culture celebrates it tells you what it treasures. When you learn that quinceañera literally holds the number fifteen, or that piñata came into English unchanged, you're seeing how language carries a community's joy. These are your neighbors' celebrations, in your neighbors' words.
