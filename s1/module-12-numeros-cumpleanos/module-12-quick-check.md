## Items

### Q1 — Recognize a small number (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Which word means **15**?
- **A) *quince*** ✅
- B) *cinco*
- C) *cincuenta*
- D) *cincuenta y cinco*

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! *quince* = 15 — the same number hiding in *quinceañera*."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Careful with the look-alikes: *cinco* = 5, *cincuenta* = 50. **Fifteen is *quince*.**"

---

### Q2 — Recognize a ten (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Which word means **40**?
- A) *catorce*
- B) *cuatro*
- **C) *cuarenta*** ✅
- D) *cincuenta*

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Correcto! *cuarenta* = 40. (*cuatro* = 4, *catorce* = 14 — easy to mix up!)"
*Feedback (incorrect):* "The tens end in **-enta**: *cuar**enta*** = 40. *cuatro* is 4 and *catorce* is 14."

---

### Q3 — Build with the *y* pattern (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** From 31 on, you connect the ten and the ones with *y* ("and"). How do you write **45**?
- **A) *cuarenta y cinco*** ✅
- B) *cincuenta y cuatro*
- C) *cuarentaycinco*
- D) *cuarenta y cuatro*

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Muy bien! 40 = *cuarenta*, then *y cinco*: **cuarenta y cinco**. Three separate words."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Build it: 40 (*cuarenta*) + *y* + 5 (*cinco*) = **cuarenta y cinco** — written as three words."

---

### Q4 — The glued teens/twenties (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** How do you write **22**?
- A) *veinte y dos*
- **B) *veintidós*** ✅
- C) *veintidiez*
- D) *dos y veinte*

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! The twenties glue into one word: **veintidós**. (The *y* split only starts at 31.)"
*Feedback (incorrect):* "16–29 are written as **one glued word**: **veintidós**. You save *y* for 31 and up."

---

### Q5 — The age chunk (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** You want to say **"I am 12 years old."** (*doce* = 12) Which is correct?
- **A) *Tengo doce años.*** ✅
- B) *Soy doce años.*
- C) *Soy doce.*
- D) *Tengo doce.*

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Perfecto! In Spanish you *have* your age: **Tengo doce años** = I am 12 years old."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Spanish doesn't use *soy* ('I am') for age, and it needs *años*. The chunk is **Tengo … años**: *Tengo doce años.*"

---

### Q6 — The question (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Someone asks you *¿Cuántos años tienes?* What do they want to know?
- **A) how old you are** ✅
- B) what your name is
- C) how many brothers you have
- D) where you are from

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Exacto! *¿Cuántos años tienes?* = 'How old are you?' — literally 'How many years do you have?'"
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*años* is the clue — they're asking your age: *¿Cuántos años tienes?* = **How old are you?**"

---

### Q7 — Read a bigger number (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** What number is *setenta y tres*?
- A) 63
- **B) 73** ✅
- C) 37
- D) 703

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! *setenta* (70) + *y tres* (3) = **73**."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Break it apart: *setenta* = 70, *y tres* = and 3 → **73**."

## Alignment notes (for the builder / reviewer)

- **7 items, gate at 70% (5/7), unlimited attempts, highest score kept** — matches the brief's gentle-gate policy (§3) and the "ambitious but relaxed" principle.
- Coverage map: Q1 recognize a small number · Q2 recognize a ten · Q3 build with *y* (31+) · Q4 glued teens/twenties (16–29) · Q5 the age chunk · Q6 the age question · Q7 read/decode a bigger number. Both number patterns and the age chunk are exercised.
- **Chunk, not paradigm.** Q5/Q6 test *tengo … años* and *¿Cuántos años tienes?* as fixed set-phrases only. The *tener* conjugation is deliberately NOT tested (that's S2). Distractors use *soy* to catch the common English-transfer error.
- **Not a culture-link checkpoint.** M12 is a numbers/age quiz; the graded English↔Spanish culture-link items live at M05, M15, M17. The *piñata / quinceañera* culture in Lesson 12.04 is taught and lightly self-checked, not formally assessed here.
- **No Tier-3 production** in M12 (the friendly first oral task was M11's *árbol de la familia*). This keeps the gentle slope.
- Correct-answer position is varied across items (A, C, A, B, A, A, B) to discourage pattern-guessing (shuffle also ON).
- Color-coding (masc = blue, fem = red) is a lesson-page feature; the Canvas quiz renders plain.
