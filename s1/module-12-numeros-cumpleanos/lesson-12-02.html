Lesson 12.02 · The counting core of the module
Count to 100 — then say your age
Learn 15 words, spot two patterns, and you can build every number to one hundred.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You don't memorize 100 numbers. You learn a handful of building blocks, then snap them together.
First the numbers you just learn (1–15). Then two easy patterns that carry you all the way to 100. Finally, drop any number into the sentence from yesterday: Tengo… años.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
say the numbers 1–15 with clean Spanish vowels.
2
build any number up to 100 using the tens plus the little word y ("and").
3
tell someone your age with the chunk Tengo… años.

📚 PART 2 · PATTERN #1 — THE TEENS AND TWENTIES

From 16 to 29, Spanish glues the numbers together into one word. Think of it as "ten-and-six," "twenty-and-three":

17 diecisiete

Notice the built-in numbers you already know hiding inside: veinticinco = 20 + 5. You don't have to memorize these one by one — just hear the pieces.

From 31 to 99, just say the ten, then y ("and"), then the ones number. Three separate words:

31 treinta y uno
42 cuarenta y dos
58 cincuenta y ocho
76 setenta y seis
99 noventa y nueve
💡 The only quirk to notice: the "y" trick starts at 31. The teens and twenties (16–29) are written as one glued-together word, but from treinta on, you split it: ten + y + ones.
🎂 PART 4 · SAYING YOUR AGE — ONE READY-MADE CHUNK

Now the payoff. To say how old you are, use this chunk and just drop your number into the blank:

Tengo doce años. = I am 12 years old.
Literally "I have 12 years." In Spanish you have your age — you don't "be" it.

And the question that asks for it:

¿Cuántos años tienes? How old are you?
Tengo trece años. I am 13.
⭐ Keep it a chunk. You'll also hear tiene… años for someone else ("Mi hermana tiene diez años" = my sister is ten). Just recognize it for now — we are not conjugating a whole verb this week. Your job is one sentence: Tengo… años.
