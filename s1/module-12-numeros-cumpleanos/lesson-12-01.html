📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Numbers are the single most useful thing you can carry into a real conversation — and Spanish numbers follow neat patterns you can learn fast.
Your age, a phone number, a price, a date, a birthday — all of it runs on numbers. This week you'll count from 1 to 100, learn the ready-made phrase tengo ___ años to say how old you are, and pick up the words for a birthday party. Today is just the warm-up: what you already know, and what's coming.

🔥 PART 1 · YOU ALREADY KNOW MORE NUMBERS THAN YOU THINK

Spanish numbers aren't strangers. Look how many of them are already sitting inside English words you know:

uno = one — a unicycle has one wheel
dos = two — a duet is two singers
tres = three — a trio, a tricycle — three

Spanish, English, and a lot of other languages all grew their number words from the same ancient roots. So when you learn uno, dos, tres, part of your brain already recognizes them. That's your head start for this week.

📚 PART 2 · BRING BACK WHAT YOU KNOW

Before we count, dust off two things from earlier modules — you'll use both this week.

  • Your five vowels (Module 1). Every number word is read with the same clean, fixed sounds: cu-a-tro, cin-co, nu-e-ve. Say every vowel.
  • Gender & the article (Module 2). The birthday words this week are colored the way you know: blue = el (masculine), red = la (feminine).
