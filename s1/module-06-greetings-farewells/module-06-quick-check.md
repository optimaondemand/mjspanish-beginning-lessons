## Items

### Q1 — Greeting by time of day (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** It is 8:00 in the morning. Which greeting fits best?
- **A) Buenos días** ✅
- B) Buenas noches
- C) Adiós
- D) Hasta mañana

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! **Buenos días** = 'Good morning'. (Notice: *buenos* is masculine — *el día*.)"
*Feedback (incorrect):* "In the morning you say **Buenos días**. *Buenas noches* is for the evening; the others are goodbyes."

---

### Q2 — Greeting by time of day (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** You see a neighbor in the evening. What do you say?
- A) Buenos días
- **B) Buenas noches** ✅
- C) Nos vemos
- D) ¿Qué tal el libro?

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Muy bien! **Buenas noches** greets someone in the evening or at night."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "In the evening/night you say **Buenas noches**. *Buenos días* is for the morning."

---

### Q3 — The gender pattern inside greetings (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Why is it *buenos* días but *buenas* tardes?
- **A) *día* is masculine (el día) and *tarde* is feminine (la tarde) — the ending matches the noun's gender** ✅
- B) It is completely random
- C) *buenos* is always used in the morning for no reason
- D) They are spelling mistakes

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Exacto! The Module 2 gender rule is at work: *el día* → **buenos**; *la tarde* → **buenas**."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "It's the gender rule from Module 2: *el día* is masculine (**buenos**), *la tarde* is feminine (**buenas**)."

---

### Q4 — "How are you?" (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Which question means **"How are you?"**
- **A) ¿Cómo estás?** ✅
- B) Mucho gusto
- C) Hasta luego
- D) Me llamo Ana

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! **¿Cómo estás?** asks 'How are you?' (the friendly, *tú* form)."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "**¿Cómo estás?** means 'How are you?'. The others are 'nice to meet you', a goodbye, and 'my name is'."

---

### Q5 — Register: tú vs. usted (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** You are talking to your **teacher**. Which "you" shows the right respect?
- A) tú
- **B) usted** ✅

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Correcto! With a teacher you use **usted** — the respectful 'you'."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "For a teacher, choose **usted** — the respectful 'you'. Save *tú* for friends and family."

---

### Q6 — Register: the safe default (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** You are not sure whether to use *tú* or *usted* with an adult you just met. What is the safe choice?
- A) *tú* — always be casual
- **B) *usted* — being a little too polite is never rude** ✅
- C) Use no "you" at all
- D) It doesn't matter

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! When in doubt, **usted**. Across Latin America it's the neutral, respectful default."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "When you're unsure, choose **usted**. Being polite is never rude — that's the safe default."

---

### Q7 — Goodbyes (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Which of these is a way to say **goodbye**?
- A) Buenos días
- B) ¿Cómo estás?
- **C) Hasta luego** ✅
- D) Mucho gusto

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Muy bien! **Hasta luego** = 'See you later'. Other goodbyes: *adiós, hasta mañana, nos vemos, chao*."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "**Hasta luego** ('see you later') is a goodbye. The others are a greeting, a question, and 'nice to meet you'."

## Alignment notes (for the builder / reviewer)

- **7 items, gate at 70% (5/7), unlimited attempts, highest score kept** — matches the brief's gentle-gate policy (§3) and the "ambitious but relaxed" principle.
- Coverage map: Q1–Q2 greetings by time of day · Q3 the buenos/buenas gender tie-in (spirals Module 2) · Q4 "how are you?" · Q5–Q6 register (tú vs usted + the safe default) · Q7 goodbyes.
- **Latin-American-first register enforced:** *usted* is the neutral respectful default and the graded answer for the "unsure" item; *vosotros* is not tested (named only as a Spain form in the lessons). No hyper-regional slang.

- Correct-answer position is varied across items (A, B, A, A, B, B, C — shuffle ON further randomizes) so students can't pattern-guess.
