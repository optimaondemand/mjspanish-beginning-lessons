Lesson 6.02 · The core of the module
The two "yous": tú and usted
One easy idea — friendly or respectful — and a safe default when you're not sure.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday's question, answered: use tú for friends and use usted to show respect — and when in doubt, usted is always polite.
You'll see how English handles this with tone instead of words, meet the two Spanish "yous," and learn a simple rule for choosing. Then you'll try it yourself.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
explain the difference between tú (friendly) and usted (respectful).
2
choose the right "you" for a friend, a teacher, or a stranger.
3
use usted as your safe default across Latin America when you're unsure.
📚 PART 1 · ENGLISH vs. SPANISH

English shows respect with your tone and extra words — you might say "Hey!" to a friend but "Good morning, sir" to a stranger. The word "you," though, never changes. It's the same "you" for everyone.

Spanish builds the respect right into the word. It gives you two different words for "you," and you pick the one that fits the person:

Words for "you"only one: "you"two: tú (friendly) & usted (respectful)

📚 PART 2 · WHO GETS WHICH "YOU"?

👋 tú — the friendly "you"

for people you're close to or equal with
  • a friend or classmate
  • a brother, sister, or cousin
  • a kid your own age
  • a pet!

🤝 usted — the respectful "you"

for people you show respect to
  • a teacher or principal
  • an adult you don't know
  • a grandparent or elder
  • a doctor, a store clerk, an official

A simple way to remember it: tú is for your circle; usted is for people you'd be polite to. You'll often hear usted written the short way, Ud.

🧠 PART 3 · WHEN YOU'RE NOT SURE

Here's the good news for a beginner: if you can't decide, choose usted. Being a little too polite is never rude — but being too casual with the wrong person can feel disrespectful. Across Latin America, usted is the neutral, respectful default that always works.

★ One quick note for later: when you talk to more than one person, Latin America uses ustedes ("you all"). In Spain, you might also hear a casual plural called vosotros — but that's a European form you'll only need if you travel there. For this course, tú, usted, and ustedes are all you need.
