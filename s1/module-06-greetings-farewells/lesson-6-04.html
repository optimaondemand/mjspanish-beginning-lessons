🌎 Spanish in Your World

Warm hellos, close by

You don't have to travel far to hear these greetings — the United States is home to tens of millions of Spanish speakers. In many Hispanic families and neighborhoods, a ¡Buenos días! to a neighbor or a ¡Hola! at the shop is part of everyday life.

Greetings also tend to be warmer and more personal than a quick English "hi." Among friends and family across Latin America, people often add a friendly handshake, a pat on the shoulder, or a light cheek kiss — and they usually take a moment to ask ¿Cómo estás? and really wait for the answer. With someone older or someone they respect, they switch to usted — exactly the choice you learned this week.

Two habits worth copying:

Greet, then talk. It can feel abrupt to jump straight to business. A quick Buenas tardes, ¿cómo está? first is polite and expected.
Match the register. Friends get tú; elders, teachers, and strangers get usted. When unsure, usted shows respect.

★ WHY THIS MATTERS

A greeting is the first thing you give another person. Learning to say buenos días to a neighbor or ¿cómo está usted? to an elder isn't just vocabulary — it's a small act of courtesy and respect that opens doors in millions of homes and communities right here in the US.
