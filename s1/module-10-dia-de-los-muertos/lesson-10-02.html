1Two old traditions that became one

El Día de los Muertos is famous in Mexico, but its roots reach back a very long way. Long before Europeans arrived, the Indigenous peoples of Mexico — including the Aztec (Nahua) civilization — already held ceremonies to remember and honor the dead. To them, death was not the end of a person's story; the people who had died were still part of the family.

Centuries later, Spanish settlers brought Catholic holy days — All Saints' Day (November 1) and All Souls' Day (November 2) — which also honor people who have died. Over time these two traditions, Indigenous and Catholic, blended together into the holiday celebrated today, held on those same November days.

2La ofrenda: an invitation home

The center of the holiday is la ofrenda — the "offering." It is a special display that families set up at home (or at the cemetery), often on a small table or altar. On it they place things their loved one enjoyed in life: photos of the person, their favorite foods, bright orange marigold flowers, candles, and colorful decorations.

Why? Families believe that during these two days, the spirits of their loved ones can return to visit. The ofrenda is a warm welcome — a way of saying, "We remember you. We saved your favorite things. We're glad you're here." The marigolds' bright color and strong scent, and the glow of the candles, are believed to help guide the spirits back home.

3A celebration, not a scary story

Here is the part that surprises many newcomers: el Día de los Muertos is joyful. Families cook, tell stories, share music, laugh, and remember the good times. The skulls you see — called calaveras — are cheerful and colorful, often made of decorated sugar, sometimes with the name of a loved one on the forehead. Nothing about the day is meant to be frightening.

That's why calling it "Mexican Halloween" misses the point. Halloween is about costumes and spooky fun. El Día de los Muertos is about love and memory — keeping the people we've lost close by celebrating who they were. Same season, completely different heart.
