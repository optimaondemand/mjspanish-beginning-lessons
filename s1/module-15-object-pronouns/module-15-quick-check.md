## Part A — Indirect object pronouns (5 items)

### Q1 — Concept (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** A word like *me* or *te* in a sentence tells you...
- A) what color something is
- **B) who *receives* the action — the "to whom / for whom"** ✅
- C) how to spell the word
- D) the time of day

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! *me, te, le, nos, les* answer 'to whom?' — the person who receives the action."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Look back at 15.02 — these little words show **who receives** the action (to me, to you, to him…)."

---

### Q2 — Placement (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** In Spanish, where does the "to-whom" word usually go?
- A) at the very end of the sentence
- **B) right before the verb** ✅
- C) after the noun
- D) it can go anywhere

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Correcto! It sits **right before the verb**: *La maestra **me** da el libro.*"
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Remember the model: *La maestra **me** da el libro.* The word goes **right before the verb**."

---

### Q3 — Identify the word (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Which little word means **"to us"**?
- A) *le*
- **B) *nos*** ✅
- C) *me*
- D) *te*

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Muy bien! *nos* = to us (you can hear it inside *nosotros*)."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*nos* = to us — it hides inside *nosotros* ('we')."

---

### Q4 — The versatile *le* (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** The word *le* can mean...
- A) only "to me"
- **B) "to him," "to her," or "to you" (usted)** ✅
- C) "to us"
- D) "to them"

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Exacto! One word, three uses: *le* = to him, to her, or to you (usted)."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*le* covers three: to him, to her, and the respectful 'to you' (usted)."

---

### Q5 — Read for meaning (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Read: **La maestra te da el taco.** Who receives the taco?
- A) me
- **B) you** ✅
- C) them
- D) us

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! *te* = to you — the teacher gives the taco to you."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "The word is *te* = to you, so **you** get the taco."

## Part B — GRADED culture-link checkpoint: loanwords (3 items)

> These are the **graded "Spanish hiding in English" items** required by the brief. They mirror the Lesson 15.04 game exactly. Verified etymologies only (build-brief §4).

### Q6 — Loanword: rodeo (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** The English word **rodeo** came from the Spanish verb *rodear*. What does *rodear* mean?
- **A) to surround / round up** ✅
- B) to ride a horse fast
- C) to sell cattle
- D) to build a fence

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! *rodear* = 'to surround / round up' — exactly what riders do with the cattle."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*rodear* means 'to surround / round up.' That is where *rodeo* comes from."

---

### Q7 — Loanword: mosquito (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** The Spanish word **mosquito** is built from *mosca* (fly) + a small ending. What does *mosquito* literally mean?
- **A) little fly** ✅
- B) big bug
- C) night biter
- D) summer pest

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Correcto! *mosca* (fly) + *-ito* (little) = 'little fly.'"
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*mosca* means 'fly,' and *-ito* means 'little' — so *mosquito* = 'little fly.'"

---

### Q8 — Loanword origin: chocolate (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** English got the word **chocolate** through Spanish. Where did Spanish borrow it from first?
- A) it was invented in Spain
- **B) from Nahuatl, a language of Mexico (*xocolatl*)** ✅
- C) from French
- D) from Latin only

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! Spanish took *chocolate* from Nahuatl *xocolatl* (a language of Mexico), then English took it from Spanish."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*chocolate* traveled into Spanish from Nahuatl *xocolatl* — a language of central Mexico — before reaching English."
