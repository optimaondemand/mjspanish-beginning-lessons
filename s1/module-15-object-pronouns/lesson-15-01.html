Who gets the book? A tiny word tells you.
Welcome to Module 15 — a short, powerful set of words you use every single day.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
"The teacher gives the book to me." How does Spanish say that little to me?
You already know a lot: how to greet people, the -o/-a gender pattern, subject pronouns, and the verbs ser and estar. This week we add one small, mighty piece: the little word that answers "to whom?" or "for whom?" — me, te, le, nos, les. No new vocabulary this week; just this one clever idea, using words you already own.

📚 PART 1 · WARM UP — WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW

You come into this week already carrying the subject pronouns (who does the action) and the verbs ser and estar. Let us dust them off.

yo — I
tú — you (a friend)
usted — you (respectful)
ella / él — she / he
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
A subject pronoun tells you who does the action. In "She is my friend," which Spanish word means "she"?
🔍 PART 2 · SOMETHING YOU ALREADY DO IN ENGLISH

A subject pronoun tells you who acts. But sentences often have a second person — the one who receives something. Read these English sentences and watch the little words in bold:

The teacher gives the book to me.
I gave the taco to you.
She wrote a letter to him.

Those bold words — to me, to you, to him — answer the question "to whom?" You use them all day without thinking. Spanish has the exact same idea, packed into one tiny word placed right before the verb. Grammar teachers call it an indirect object pronoun, but you can just think of it as the "to-whom" word.

💡 The big question of the week: if English says "the teacher gives the book to me," where do you think Spanish puts its "to-whom" word — at the end, like English, or somewhere else? Hold onto your hunch. Tomorrow it all clicks.
