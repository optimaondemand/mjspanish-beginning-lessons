Lesson 15.02 · The heart of the module
The five "to-whom" words — and where they go
One short list, one placement rule. That is the whole idea.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday's question, answered: the "to-whom" word in Spanish is one small word, and it goes right before the verb.
First you will see how English and Spanish arrange this differently, then learn the five little words — me, te, le, nos, les — and finally practice with sentences made only from words you already know.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
list the five "to-whom" words: me, te, le, nos, les.
2
place the word correctly — right before the verb.
3
read a short sentence and say who receives the action.
📚 PART 1 · ENGLISH vs. SPANISH — WHERE THE WORD GOES

In English, the "to-whom" words usually come after the verb, often with the word "to": gives the book to me. Spanish does it more simply. It uses one small word and drops it in right before the verb — no "to" needed.

 EnglishSpanish
The ideathe person who receives the actionsame idea — the person who receives the action
How it looksoften two words: "to me," "to you"one small word: me, te
Where it goesusually after the verbright before the verb
ExampleThe teacher gives the book to me.La maestra me da el libro.
💡 Look at the example again: La maestra me da el libro. The little me sits just before da (gives). Word for word it is "The teacher to-me gives the book." That front placement feels strange at first — and then it feels normal fast.
📚 PART 2 · THE FIVE "TO-WHOM" WORDS

Here is the whole set. Notice that a few of them look like words you already know (nos is hiding inside nosotros). This is the list to learn this week:

WordMeans "to / for…"
meto / for me
teto / for you (a friend)
leto / for him, her, or you (usted)
nosto / for us
lesto / for them or you all
THE ONE MODEL TO REMEMBER
La maestra me da el libro.
The teacher gives the book to me. — the "to-whom" word sits before the verb da.

Swap only the little word and you change who receives the book: me da (to me), te da (to you), le da (to him/her), nos da (to us), les da (to them). The verb and the noun stay put — only the "to-whom" word moves.

✅ PART 3 · TRY IT — WHO RECEIVES IT?
