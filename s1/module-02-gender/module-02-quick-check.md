## Items

### Q1 — Concept (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** In English there is just one word for "the." How many words for "the" did Module 2 teach you in Spanish?
- A) One — Spanish works the same as English
- **B) Two — *el* and *la*** ✅
- C) None — Spanish has no word for "the"
- D) They are the same word spelled two ways

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! Spanish uses **el** and **la** (and later *los* and *las*). Which one depends on the noun's gender."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Not quite — Module 2's big idea is that Spanish has **two** words for 'the': *el* and *la*."

---

### Q2 — The core rule (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** A Spanish noun ends in **-o**. Which article most likely goes with it?
- **A) *el* (masculine)** ✅
- B) *la* (feminine)
- C) It's completely random
- D) Both are always correct

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Correcto! **-o → el (masculine)**. That's half of the pattern."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Remember the pattern: a word ending in **-o** is usually **masculine → el**."

---

### Q3 — The core rule, other half (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** A Spanish noun ends in **-a**. Which article most likely goes with it?
- A) *el* (masculine)
- **B) *la* (feminine)** ✅
- C) It depends on the day
- D) Neither

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Muy bien! **-a → la (feminine)**. Now you have both halves of the rule."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Almost — a word ending in **-a** is usually **feminine → la**."

---

### Q4 — Apply the rule (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Choose the correct article: **_____ libro** (*libro* = book; it ends in **-o**).
- **A) el libro** ✅
- B) la libro

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Perfecto! *libro* ends in -o → **el libro**."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*libro* ends in **-o**, so it's masculine: **el libro**."

---

### Q5 — Apply the rule (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Choose the correct article: **_____ fiesta** (a party — you met this Spanish word in Module 1).
- A) el fiesta
- **B) la fiesta** ✅

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! *fiesta* ends in -a → **la fiesta**."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*fiesta* ends in **-a**, so it's feminine: **la fiesta**."

---

### Q6 — Exception awareness (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** The -o/-a pattern works most of the time, but a few words break it. Which of these is an **exception** — a word whose article does NOT match its ending?
- A) *la casa* (ends in -a, takes *la* — follows the rule)
- B) *el taco* (ends in -o, takes *el* — follows the rule)
- **C) *la mano* (ends in -o, but takes *la*)** ✅
- D) *el patio* (ends in -o, takes *el* — follows the rule)

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Exacto! ***la mano*** (the hand) ends in -o but is feminine — one of the few exceptions, along with *el día* and *el mapa*."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Look for the mismatch: **la mano** ends in -o but takes *la*. That's the exception. The others all follow the rule."

---

### Q7 — Cognate / culture link (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** You read *la música* in a Spanish song title and understand it right away. Module 2 called a word like this — a Spanish word that looks and means almost the same as an English word — a:
- **A) cognate (here, a cousin of English *music*)** ✅
- B) an exception to the gender rule
- C) a word you must memorize from scratch
- D) a farewell expression

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! *música* is a **cognate** of *music* — free comprehension. And it ends in -a, so it takes *la*: **la música**."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*música* is a **cognate** — it's the cousin of English *music*, so you understand it for free. (It also follows the rule: -a → la.)"

## Alignment notes (for the builder / reviewer)

- **7 items, gate at 70% (5/7), unlimited attempts, highest score kept** — matches the brief's gentle-gate policy (§3) and the "ambitious but relaxed" principle.
- Coverage map: Q1 concept · Q2–Q3 the rule (both halves) · Q4–Q5 apply the rule · Q6 exceptions · Q7 cognate/culture link. Every M02 objective is assessed.
- **No new vocabulary is tested** — all nouns are M01 loanwords (*fiesta*, *taco*, *patio*), transparent cognates (*música*, *casa*), or glossed in-item (*libro*). Assessment targets the gender concept, not memory (gentle slope; "new grammar on known vocab").
- Correct-answer position is varied across items (B, A, B, A, B, C, A) so students can't pattern-guess.
- This is **not** one of the S1 graded culture-link checkpoints (those are M05, M15, M17). Q7 is a light cognate item for reinforcement, not a formal checkpoint.
- Color-coding (masc = blue, fem = red) is a lesson-page feature; the Canvas quiz renders plain. The concepts transfer without it.
