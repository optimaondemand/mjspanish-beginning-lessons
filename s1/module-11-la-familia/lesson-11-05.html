📝 THE WHOLE WEEK ON ONE CARD
The pattern. Male family word ends in -o → el. Female ends in -a → la. Flip the ending, flip the person.
The special pair. el padre / la madre end in -e — the article, not the ending, tells you who.
The words. hermano/hermana, hijo/hija, abuelo/abuela, tío/tía, primo/prima — plus la familia itself.
Saying "my." mi works for anyone: mi hermano, mi abuela. Point people out with este es / esta es.

🌱 YOUR PROJECT · EL ÁRBOL DE LA FAMILIA (FAMILY TREE)

This is your first speaking project — and it's a friendly one. You'll make a simple family tree and record a short spoken tour of it. You may read from your notes, and you can practice as many times as you like before you submit.

1
Draw a simple family tree — on paper, in a slide, or in a document. Include 4–6 family members (real or invented) with their names. Label each with the Spanish word and el/la.
2
Write your notes. For each person, plan a sentence like: Esta es mi madre. Se llama Ana. / Este es mi hermano. Se llama Luis.
3
Record yourself using the media recorder in the Canvas assignment. Speak for 30–90 seconds, introducing each family member. Hold up your tree or share your screen if you like.
4
Submit the recording. Your teacher will listen and give you feedback with a simple 4-point rubric — graded on completeness and effort, not perfection.
💬 Sentence starters you can use: En mi familia hay… (In my family there is/are…) · Este es mi… (This is my… — male) · Esta es mi… (This is my… — female) · Se llama… (His/her name is…)
