LESSON 11.04 · SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD
Family words you already hear
These aren't distant, foreign words — you've probably heard them at a neighbor's house or in a movie.
🌍 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
In the United States, Spanish family words are everywhere — in homes, in music, and in everyday nicknames.

Millions of families in the U.S. speak Spanish at home, so words like abuela, tía, and mamá are woven right into American life. You may already know some of them — and now you know the grammar behind them, too.

✨ WHERE YOU'VE HEARD THEM
la abuela · el abuelo
Grandma and Grandpa. In many U.S. Spanish-speaking families, kids call them abuela and abuelo — or the affectionate short forms abuelita / abuelito.
mija · mijo
A warm nickname a parent uses for a child. It's simply mi hija ("my daughter") and mi hijo ("my son") said fast — the words you learned yesterday, squeezed together.
mamá · papá
The everyday, affectionate words for Mom and Dad. Both carry an accent on that last vowel — say them with the stress at the end: ma-MÁ, pa-PÁ. They're the warm cousins of el padre and la madre.
★ WHY THIS MATTERS

Family is at the heart of Spanish-speaking cultures — and the language shows it, with warm words like mija and abuelita that carry real affection. When you learn family vocabulary, you're not just memorizing a list; you're learning how millions of your neighbors talk to the people they love.

🎮 PLAY · FAMILY MEMORY MATCH

Flip two cards to match each Spanish family word with the right person. Matches stay face-up. Play until you clear the board.

📖 READ · MI FAMILIA

Here's a short introduction to a family. You know every word — read it out loud, then answer below.

¡Hola! Me llamo Lucía. En mi familia hay cinco personas. Mi madre se llama Rosa y mi padre se llama Miguel. Tengo un hermano, Diego, y una hermana, Sofía. Mi abuela Carmen también vive con nosotros. ¡Es una familia grande y feliz!
✅ APPLY IT · DID YOU FOLLOW THE FAMILY?
Use the reading above.
Who is Rosa?
Diego is Lucía's hermano and Sofía is her hermana. What does that make Sofía?
The reading says hay cinco personas. What does that tell you?
