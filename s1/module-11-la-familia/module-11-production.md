# Module 11 — Production Task: El árbol de la familia (Tier-3, teacher-graded)

**Course:** M/J Spanish, Beginning (0708000) · Semester 1
**Module:** M11 — La familia
**Task name:** El árbol de la familia (The Family Tree)
**Tier:** Tier 3 — teacher-graded production (counts toward "Projects & Oral Production" weight)
**Submission type:** **Canvas media recorder** (record audio *or* video in-browser — no external app)
**Placement note:** This is the S1 family-tree task, **moved early to M11** per the reconciled build brief (§2) — deliberately positioned as the friendly *first* speaking project of the course.

---

## What the student does

Students build a simple family tree (real or invented) and record a short spoken tour of it, introducing 4–6 family members by name and relationship. **Presentational, not interpersonal** — the student speaks to the camera/mic, no live conversation partner → FERPA-clean.

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Format | **Canvas media recorder** submission (audio or video, in-browser) |
| Length | **30–90 seconds** |
| Register | Latin-American-neutral; warm, personal |
| Notes | **Read-from-notes allowed** — students may glance at written notes / their labeled tree |
| Attempts | Students may re-record freely before submitting; submit their best take |
| Points | **60 pts**, mapped from the 4-point rubric below (gentled major, per §3) |
| Scaffolding | Fully scaffolded on lesson 11.05 (steps + sentence starters); this is a low-stress first major |

### Student instructions (paste into the Canvas assignment)

> **El árbol de la familia — your first speaking project! 🌳**
>
> 1. **Make a simple family tree** — on paper, in a slide, or in a doc. Include **4–6 family members** (real or invented), each with a name.
> 2. **Label each person in Spanish** with the correct article: *el padre*, *la madre*, *mi hermano*, etc.
> 3. **Plan a sentence for each person**, for example: *Esta es mi madre. Se llama Ana.* / *Este es mi hermano. Se llama Luis.*
> 4. **Record yourself** with the media recorder below — speak for **30–90 seconds**, introducing each family member. You may hold up your tree, share your screen, or just describe it. **You can read from your notes** and re-record as many times as you want.
> 5. **Submit** your best take. 
>
> **Sentence starters:** *En mi familia hay…* · *Este es mi…* (male) · *Esta es mi…* (female) · *Se llama…* · *Tengo un hermano / una hermana…*
>
> This is graded on **completeness and effort**, not on being perfect. ¡Tú puedes! (You can do it!)

---

## 4-point rubric (teacher-graded in SpeedGrader)

Four criteria, each scored **1–4**. Total 4–16 raw → scaled to 60 pts (raw ÷ 16 × 60, rounded). A blank/absent submission = 0.

| Criterion | 4 — Exceeds | 3 — Meets | 2 — Approaching | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Completeness** (4–6 members introduced) | 5–6 family members clearly introduced by name + relationship | 4 members introduced by name + relationship | 2–3 members, or relationships unclear | Fewer than 2, or no clear family members |
| **2. Family vocabulary + gender** (correct family words with *el/la/mi*) | Family words used accurately with correct gender throughout | Mostly correct; 1–2 gender/word slips | Several slips, but meaning still comes through | Family words mostly missing or incorrect |
| **3. Pronunciation & delivery** (clean Spanish vowels, audible) | Clear, steady Spanish vowels; easy to understand | Understandable; a few English-vowel habits | Frequent slips but still followable | Hard to understand / mostly inaudible |
| **4. Effort & length** (30–90 s, evident preparation) | Full 30–90 s, well-prepared, confident | Meets length; prepared | Short or under-rehearsed | Very short / no evident preparation |

**Feedback guidance for the teacher:** Lead with one specific thing the student did well (a clean *la abuela*, a confident intro), then one gentle next step. Keep it encouraging — this is the first oral production of the course.
