Lesson 11.02 · The grammar core of the module
One ending, two people — the family pattern
This is the rule that makes half the family vocabulary "two for one."
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
For most family words, learn one and you get its partner for free: just flip -o to -a.
You'll see the pattern laid out, meet the one famous pair that breaks it (padre / madre), and learn how the article (el / la) rides along with the person. No new vocabulary to cram — this is about the pattern.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
make the female family word from the male one (and back) by switching -o and -a.
2
choose the right article, el or la, for a family member.
3
recognize the special pair el padre / la madre, which don't use -o/-a.
📚 PART 1 · THE PATTERN THAT PAIRS PEOPLE UP

With family members, the -o/-a ending does something wonderful: it tells you male or female. So most family words come in a matched pair. Learn the -o version and the -a version is basically free.

♂ Male → ends in -o → el

Blue, all year. Male family member.
  • el hermano brother
  • el hijo son
  • el abuelo grandfather
  • el tío uncle
  • el primo cousin (male)

♀ Female → ends in -a → la

Red, all year. Female family member.
  • la hermana sister
  • la hija daughter
  • la abuela grandmother
  • la tía aunt
  • la prima cousin (female)

See how each row lines up? hermano → hermana, tío → tía. Change one letter, change the person — and the article changes to match: el for the male, la for the female.

⭐ PART 2 · THE ONE PAIR THAT PLAYS BY ITS OWN RULES

There is one very important pair — the two people at the top of most families — that does not use the tidy -o/-a switch. You just learn them as a pair:

el padre the father (ends in -e, but male)
la madre the mother (ends in -e, but female)

Notice they both end in -e, not -o or -a — so the ending doesn't tell you male or female this time. But the article still does the job: el padre is a father, la madre is a mother. In everyday speech you'll also hear the warmer, casual words mamá (mom) and papá (dad) — those do follow the sound pattern.

💡 Handy set-phrases: when you mean the whole group, Spanish often uses the masculine plural: los padres can mean "the parents," los abuelos "the grandparents," and los hermanos "the siblings." You'll meet plural articles fully later — for now just recognize these three as useful chunks.
🧠 PART 3 · SAYING "MY" AND "THE" WITH FAMILY

To talk about your own family, put the little word mi ("my") in front — and here's a relief: mi looks the same for a male or a female.

mi hermano — my brother
mi hermana — my sister
mi madre — my mother

And when you point someone out, you'll use este es… ("this is…" a male) or esta es… ("this is…" a female) — same -o ish / -a feel you already know. You'll practice these in your family-tree recording on Friday.

✅ PART 4 · CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

Just practice — tap around and learn from the feedback. Make sure the pattern landed before you go on.

💪 ONE IDEA TO CARRY OUT

Most family words pair up: flip -o to -a and you switch from a male relative to a female one. The only pair to memorize on its own is el padre / la madre.
