Meet la familia
Welcome to Module 11 — the week you talk about the people closest to you.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You already know the secret this whole module runs on — you learned it back in Module 2.
Family words are the friendliest place in all of Spanish to see grammatical gender, because with people the ending usually matches whether we mean a male or a female: hermano is a brother, hermana is a sister. This week you'll build your whole familia in Spanish — and at the end, record a short tour of your family tree.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
bring back the -o / -a gender pattern and the el / la articles from Module 2.
2
notice that with people, the ending often tells you male or female — a boy vs. a girl.
3
preview the week and the friendly project at the end: el árbol de la familia.
📚 PART 1 · WARM UP — WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW

Back in Module 2 you learned the pattern that unlocks this whole week: a word ending in -o usually takes el and is masculine; a word ending in -a usually takes la and is feminine. You even saw it with people:

el niño a boy
la niña a girl
el amigo a male friend
la amiga a female friend
✅ QUICK RECALL · USE YOUR MODULE 2 RULE
No new words yet — just dust off the pattern.
The word hermano (brother) ends in -o. Which article goes with it?
The word hermana (sister) ends in -a. Which article goes with it?
🔍 PART 2 · SOMETHING WORTH NOTICING

Look at hermano and hermana again. Same word — only the last letter changes. That one little switch flips the meaning from brother to sister. Family words do this all the time:

hijo son
hija daughter
tío uncle
tía aunt
💡 The big idea of the week: with people, that -o/-a ending isn't just grammar — it tells you who you mean, a male family member or a female one. Tomorrow you'll learn the pattern in full (and meet one special pair that plays by its own rules).
