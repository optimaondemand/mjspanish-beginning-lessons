LESSON 3.02 · THE GRAMMAR THAT RUNS THE ROOM
Commands: language that tells you to do
Your only job this week is to understand and respond — not to produce these yet.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A Spanish teacher runs the whole room on about eight little words. Learn to follow them and you can survive a real Spanish class from day one.
A command is a special verb form that gives an instruction — "Listen," "Look," "Open the book." You already respond to English commands without thinking. Today you'll learn the Spanish ones by hearing them and doing what they say. This is called TPR — Total Physical Response — and it's the fastest, calmest way in.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
recognize the eight most common classroom commands when you hear or read them.
2
do the right action when you get a command — without translating word-by-word.
3
explain why these are the respectful ("usted") forms — the neutral default across Latin America.
📚 PART 1 · WHAT IS A COMMAND?

Most sentences describe: "The book is on the table." A command is different — it directs: "Open the book." No "you," no "please" required; the verb itself carries the order. English does it too: Listen. Look. Sit down.

Spanish has a polite, respectful way to give these — the usted command. Remember from Module 1: across most of Latin America, usted is the normal, respectful default for a teacher or an adult. So the commands a teacher gives students come out in this neutral, courteous form. You'll hear the verbs end in a clean -e or -a sound: Escuche, Mire, Lea, Abra.

💡 Gentle promise: you do not have to build these forms yourself this week. That comes much later. Right now you only need to recognize each one and do what it says.
