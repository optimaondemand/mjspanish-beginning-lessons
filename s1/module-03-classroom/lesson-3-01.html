LESSON 3.01
¡Bienvenido a la clase!
A warm-up for the week — a quick look back, then a look at everything you'll be able to name and do by Friday.
👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
The classroom is the one place you spend hours in every day — so it's the perfect place to start naming the world in Spanish.
This week you'll learn the Spanish words for the things around you at school, and you'll start to understand and follow the little commands a Spanish teacher gives all day — "listen," "look," "open your book." Nothing to memorize today: we just warm up and preview the week.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS PAGE YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
recall the two big ideas you already own: the five fixed vowels, and el / la gender.
2
name what "a command" is — language that tells you to do something.
3
preview the week and know what's coming each day.
📚 PART 1 · LOOK AROUND YOU

Wherever you're sitting right now, look up for a second. A desk. A book. A pencil. A door. A window. In an in-person Spanish class, the room itself becomes the lesson — the teacher points and names things, tells you to open your book or look at the board, and you just do it. You learn without even translating.

That's exactly what this week does. By Friday you'll be able to name the objects around you in Spanish and follow a handful of classroom instructions — the same ones a teacher uses every single day.

🧠 PART 2 · QUICK RECALL — YOU ALREADY OWN THESE

Before we add anything new, let's dust off the two tools you built in Modules 1 and 2. You'll use both all week.

Module 1 · The five vowels
a-e-i-o-u — five sounds that never change. You'll read every new word this week with them.
Module 2 · el / la
Ends in -o → usually el. Ends in -a → usually la. Every classroom word this week is color-coded the same way.
✅ WARM UP YOUR MEMORY
Two quick ones from last week — just to prove it stuck.
The word mesa (table) ends in -a. Which article goes with it?
In the word libro (book), how do you pronounce the letter i?
💡 PART 3 · A NEW KIND OF SENTENCE: THE COMMAND

You already know English commands, even if you've never called them that. When a coach says "Run!" or a sign says "Push," that's a command — language that tells someone to do something. It doesn't describe; it directs.

Spanish classrooms run on a small set of these: Escuche (Listen), Mire (Look), Abra el libro (Open the book). This week your job is to understand and respond to them — not to produce them yet. Just like a real first day, you'll learn to do before you learn to say. Tomorrow you meet the commands themselves.

★ A WORD ON RESPONSIBILITY

Learning to listen and respond to instructions in another language takes a small kind of self-government — slowing down, paying attention, doing the thing before rushing ahead. That patience is exactly what makes the rest of the year easier.
