Lesson 16.02 · The heart of the module
The backwards verb, made simple
One idea, one small choice: gusta or gustan. That is it.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday's question, answered: gustar means "to be pleasing," so the thing you like decides the ending.
We will practice only on words you already know — la música, los tacos, la pizza, el chocolate, el fútbol. New grammar, familiar vocabulary. One like, one word: gusta. More than one, or a plural: gustan.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
explain why gustar is "backwards" — the thing liked is the star.
2
choose gusta for one thing and gustan for more than one.
3
build a sentence: me/te/le + gusta/gustan + the thing.
📚 PART 1 · WHY IT IS "BACKWARDS"

In English, you do the liking: "I like pizza." In Spanish, the verb gustar really means "to be pleasing" — so the sentence turns around. The thing becomes the star, and you are pointed to with a to-whom word:

Me gusta la pizza.
word for word: "To me, pizza is pleasing" — which is how Spanish says "I like pizza."

Because the thing is the star, the ending of gustar matches the thing, not you. That is the one choice you make every time — and it is easy.

📚 PART 2 · THE ONE CHOICE: gusta OR gustan

① ONE thing → gusta

The thing you like is singular (or an action). Use gusta.
  • Me gusta la música.
  • Me gusta la pizza.
  • Me gusta bailar. (to dance)

② MORE than one → gustan

The thing you like is plural (ends in -s). Use gustan.
  • Me gustan los tacos.
  • Me gustan los deportes. (sports)
  • Me gustan las fiestas.
💡 The trick: look at the thing, not at yourself. One thing (or an activity) → gusta. Two or more → gustan. And to talk about liking to do something, always use gusta + the action: Me gusta cantar (I like to sing).
