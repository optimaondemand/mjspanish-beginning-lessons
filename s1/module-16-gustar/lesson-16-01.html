📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You are about to unlock one of the most-used verbs in all of Spanish: gustar — "to like."
Here is the good news: you already did the hard part last week. Gustar is built right on top of the little to-whom words — me, te, le — you just learned. Today we warm those up and get ready to say what you like.

📚 PART 1 · WARM UP — LAST WEEK'S WORDS

Module 15 gave you the little "to-whom" words. This week they come right back — because gustar cannot work without them. Let us refresh the three you will use most:

me — to me
te — to you
le — to him / her / usted
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
If me means "to me," what do you think me gusta is getting at?
🔍 PART 2 · A VERB THAT WORKS BACKWARDS

In English you say "I like tacos." I am the subject, doing the liking. Spanish flips this around. It does not say "I like tacos." It says something closer to:

Me gustan los tacos.
word for word: "To me, tacos are pleasing."

Strange, right? Instead of "I like the tacos," Spanish says "the tacos are pleasing to me." That is why we call gustar a backwards verb — the thing you like is really the star of the sentence, and you are the one it points to with the little to-whom word.

💡 The big question of the week: if gustar really means "to be pleasing," then what decides whether we say gusta or gustan? Hold onto your hunch — tomorrow the whole pattern clicks into place.

💪 ONE IDEA TO CARRY OUT

Spanish does not say "I like tacos." It says "tacos are pleasing to me" — Me gustan los tacos. That little backwards twist is the whole trick, and last week's to-whom words make it easy.
