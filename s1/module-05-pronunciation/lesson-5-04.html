Lesson 5.04 · Spanish in Your World
Your map is written in Spanish
The US states and cities you've said your whole life are Spanish words — with real meanings.
🌍 TODAY'S FOCUS
Colorado, Nevada, Amarillo, Florida — these aren't just names. Each one is a Spanish word that describes a place.
Spanish explorers and settlers named huge parts of what is now the United States. When they saw a red river or a snowy mountain range, they described it — in Spanish. Those descriptions became the names on your map. And now that you can pronounce Spanish, you can read them.
🌎 Spanish in Your World

Names that are really descriptions

Think about how the names sound now that you know your consonants: Nevada starts with that Spanish "b"-style v, and Colorado and Amarillo roll off cleanly with steady vowels. Say each one the Spanish way and its meaning almost jumps out.

Here's a first look — each place name paired with the everyday Spanish word inside it:

Colorado — colorado, "reddish / red-colored" (the red river)
Nevada — nevada, "snow-covered" (the Sierra Nevada)
Amarillo — amarillo, "yellow"
Florida — florida, "flowery"
Las Vegas — las vegas, "the meadows"
Montana — montaña, "mountain"

★ WHY THIS MATTERS

Spanish didn't arrive in the United States recently — it has been here for centuries, long enough to name the land itself. Every time you say "Colorado" or "Florida," you're already speaking a little Spanish. Learning the language is just uncovering a story your own map has been telling all along.
