A few Spanish sounds that surprise English speakers
Welcome to Module 5 — the week your Spanish starts to sound Spanish.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You already own the five vowels and the alphabet. This week we polish a handful of consonants that behave differently than you'd expect.
In Modules 1–4 you learned the vowels, the alphabet, and the letters r and rr. Today we warm those up, then notice something strange: in Spanish, the letters b and v make the same sound. That's your first clue that a few consonants play by their own rules — and this week you'll master them.

🔍 PART 2 · SOMETHING SURPRISING

Say these two English words out loud: berry and very. Your lips do something different, right? For berry your lips press together; for very your top teeth touch your bottom lip. English keeps b and v as two separate sounds.

Here's the surprise: Spanish does not. In Spanish, the letters b and v make the exact same sound — the "b" one, with the lips. So these two words sound identical to a Spanish speaker:

la vaca (the cow)
la boca (the mouth)

Different first letters — v and b — but the same opening sound: "BAH." A Spanish speaker never says a "teeth-on-lip" v. This is one of the most useful things to know for reading aloud, and it trips up almost every English speaker at first.

💡 The big idea of the week: a few Spanish consonants don't match your English instincts — b and v are twins, d and t are softer than in English, and s, z, and soft c all share one sound. Master these five and you'll sound Spanish, not just read it.
