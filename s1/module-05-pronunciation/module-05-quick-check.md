## Section A — Pronunciation (Q1–Q3)

### Q1 — B / V (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** In Spanish, how do the letters **b** and **v** sound?
- **A) Exactly the same — both make a "b" sound with the lips** ✅
- B) Different, just like English *b* and *v*
- C) The *v* is always silent
- D) Both sound like an English *w*

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! In Spanish, **b** and **v** are twins — one shared 'b' sound. That's why *vaca* and *boca* begin alike."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Remember Module 5's first surprise: Spanish **b** and **v** make the *same* 'b' sound."

---

### Q2 — S / Z / C (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** In Latin American Spanish, the letters **s**, **z**, and **c** (before *e* or *i*) all make which sound?
- A) A "th" sound, like in *think*
- **B) One plain "s" sound** ✅
- C) A hard "k" sound
- D) A "sh" sound

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Correcto! One clean 's' for all three — so *casa* and *caza* sound alike. (The 'th' version is a Spain accent; we use the Latin American 's'.)"
*Feedback (incorrect):* "In this course we use the Latin American sound: **s, z, and soft c all say a plain 's'.** The 'th' is a regional Spain accent."

---

### Q3 — D / T (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** A Spanish **t** is different from an English **t** because it is said…
- A) With a big puff of air
- **B) Gently, with the tongue on the teeth and no puff of air** ✅
- C) With a rolling sound
- D) Silently

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Muy bien! Spanish **t** (and **d**) are soft, on the teeth, with no puff of air — that gentleness is the Spanish signature."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "Spanish **t** has *no* puff of air and sits on the teeth — much gentler than the English 't'."

## Section B — Place-name culture-link (Q4–Q8) · GRADED CHECKPOINT

*(Drawn straight from Lesson 5.04's "Spanish in Your World" guess game and the Section-4 verified place-name bank.)*

### Q4 — Colorado (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** The US state **Colorado** is a Spanish word. What does *colorado* mean?
- A) cold
- **B) reddish / red-colored** ✅
- C) golden

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! *colorado* = 'reddish' — named for the red, muddy Colorado River."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*colorado* means 'reddish' — Spanish explorers named the red Colorado River."

---

### Q5 — Nevada (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** **Nevada** (and the *Sierra Nevada* mountains) is Spanish for…
- **A) snow-covered / snowy** ✅
- B) never
- C) new land

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Correcto! *nevada* = 'snow-covered' — for the snowy Sierra Nevada range."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*nevada* means 'snow-covered' — think of the snowy Sierra Nevada."

---

### Q6 — Amarillo (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** **Amarillo**, Texas is simply a Spanish color word. Which color?
- A) orange
- **B) yellow** ✅
- C) brown

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Sí! *amarillo* = 'yellow' — a color word you'll use all year."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*amarillo* is the Spanish word for 'yellow'."

---

### Q7 — Florida (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Your home state, **Florida**, got its Spanish name because *florida* means…
- A) flat land
- **B) flowery / full of flowers** ✅
- C) far south

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Exacto! *florida* = 'flowery' — named around *Pascua Florida*, the 'flowery' Easter season, in 1513."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*florida* means 'flowery' — named by Ponce de León around the 'flowery' Easter season."

---

### Q8 — Las Vegas / Montana (Multiple Choice)
**Prompt:** Which US place name means **"the meadows"** in Spanish?
- **A) Las Vegas** ✅
- B) Montana (that one means "mountain")
- C) Sacramento (that one means "sacrament")

*Feedback (correct):* "¡Muy bien! *las vegas* = 'the meadows' — the green, watered spots travelers found in the desert."
*Feedback (incorrect):* "*Las Vegas* means 'the meadows'. (*Montana* = 'mountain'; *Sacramento* = 'sacrament'.)"
