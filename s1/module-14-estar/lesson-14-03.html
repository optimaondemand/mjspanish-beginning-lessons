LESSON 14.03 · THE ONE IDEA THAT DECIDES IT
SER vs. ESTAR
Two verbs for "to be" — one simple question tells you which to use.
🔥 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
Ask yourself one question: am I saying who/what something is, or how it feels / where it is?

English uses one verb ("to be") for both, so this choice is brand new for you. But it's not a guessing game — there's a clean line between the two verbs. Learn the line once, and you'll pick the right verb almost every time.

📚 PART 1 · TWO VERBS, TWO JOBS

Here is the whole idea on one screen. Read both columns out loud:

SER

Who or what something IS — the identity, the lasting facts.
  • name — Yo soy Ana.
  • where you're from — Soy de México.
  • your role/job — Ella es doctora.

ESTAR

How something FEELS or WHERE it is — the right-now stuff.
  • how you feel — Estoy bien.
  • where a thing/place is — Miami está en Florida.
  • a mood right now — Estoy cansado.
💡 A memory trick some students like: ESTAR is for how you feel and where you are — both start with a letter you can hook to ESTAR's "e." SER is for the deeper "this is who I am." When in doubt, ask: a lasting fact (SER), or a right-now condition or place (ESTAR)?
🔊 PART 2 · FEELINGS RIDE WITH ESTAR

Here are three feelings to use with ESTAR. Since they describe a person, the ending matches you: a boy says the -o form, a girl the -a form — just like amigo / amiga back in Module 2. Press ▶ and say the one that fits you:

contento / contenta happy
cansado / cansada tired
nervioso / nerviosa nervous

Put them together with ESTAR: Yo estoy contento (a boy: I'm happy) · Yo estoy cansada (a girl: I'm tired). Because a feeling can change hour to hour, it's ESTAR — never SER.

🎮 PLAY · SORT IT: SER or ESTAR?

Read each idea and drag it to the verb it needs. Ask yourself: a lasting fact, or a right-now feeling/place? Correct drops lock in green; wrong ones bounce back.
