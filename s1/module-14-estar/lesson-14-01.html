LESSON 14.01 · A FAMILIAR SHAPE, A NEW JOB
Spanish has a second verb for "to be"
You already own SER. This week you meet its twin — and learn the one idea that tells them apart.
👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
English has one verb for "to be." Spanish has two — SER and ESTAR — and each one has its own job.

Last week you learned SER for saying who and what you are: soy estudiante, ella es doctora. This week you meet ESTAR. It also means "to be," but it's used for different things — mostly how you feel and where things are. Don't worry: learning the second verb is easier because you already know how conjugation works.

🔄 FIRST · WARM UP YOUR SER

Before we add a new verb, let's make sure last week's is solid. Fill in each form of SER in your head, then check yourself:

  • Yo soy — I am
  • Tú eres — you are
  • Ella es — she is
  • Nosotros somos — we are
  • Ellos son — they are

All five come back easily? Good — because ESTAR follows the very same idea. Here's the sneak peek that makes the whole week click:

SER — who / what you are

Yo soy estudiante. (I am a student.) Identity, origin, the permanent stuff.

ESTAR — how & where you are

Yo estoy bien. (I am well.) Miami está en Florida. Feelings and location.

🎯 BY THE END OF THIS WEEK YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
say all five forms of ESTAR (estoy, estás, está, estamos, están).
2
choose SER or ESTAR — identity vs. feeling/location — without overthinking it.
3
use ESTAR to say where a place is — and reread the US map you already decoded.
