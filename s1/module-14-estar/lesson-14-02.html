LESSON 14.02 · NEW FORMS, FAMILIAR IDEA
The Five Forms of ESTAR
Same conjugation idea as SER — just a new set of five shapes to learn.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
ESTAR means "to be," and it conjugates just like SER did — one shape for each "who."

You'll practice these on words you already know — a couple of feelings and the word aquí (here). No new vocabulary to juggle today: the only new thing is the five ESTAR shapes. Learn the forms now; next lesson you'll learn exactly when to reach for ESTAR instead of SER.

🔊 PART 1 · ESTAR, FORM BY FORM — ESCUCHA Y REPITE

Press ▶ on each row and say the form back out loud. Notice they all start with est- — that shared start makes them easy to spot:

WhoESTARMeaningListen
yoestoyI am
túestásyou are (familiar)
usted / él / ellaestáyou are (polite) / he is / she is
nosotros / nosotrasestamoswe are
ustedes / ellos / ellasestányou all are / they are
💡 Tip: just like SER, one form — está — covers usted, él, and ella. And the little accent mark on está, estás, están tells you to push the stress onto that last part: es-TÁ.
💬 PART 2 · TRY THEM IN A SENTENCE

Snap each form onto a word you already know — bien (well), aquí (here). You're not learning new vocabulary, just new verb shapes:

  • Yo estoy bien. — I am well.
  • ¿Cómo estás? — How are you? (you already say this!)
  • Ella está aquí. — She is here.
  • Nosotros estamos bien. — We are well.
  • Ellos están aquí. — They are here.

See that? ¿Cómo estás? — the greeting you've used since Module 6 — was ESTAR all along. You've been conjugating this verb without knowing its name.

🎮 PLAY · MATCH THE PERSON TO THE ESTAR FORM

Match each "who" with its ESTAR form. Correct pairs lock in; clear the whole board.

✅ TRY IT · PICK THE RIGHT ESTAR FORM
Use the table above. Read the feedback either way — you can't lose.

➡️ ONE IDEA TO CARRY OUT · WHAT'S NEXT

ESTAR has five shapes, all starting with est-: estoy, estás, está, estamos, están. Say them once more before you close the page.
