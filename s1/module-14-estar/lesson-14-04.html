LESSON 14.04 · SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD
Where is it? — ESTAR on the US map
You decoded these Spanish place names back in Module 5. Now you can say where they are.
🌍 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
Saying where a place is is an ESTAR job — and the places on your map already have Spanish names you understand.

Remember Module 5? You learned that Colorado means "reddish," Nevada means "snowy," and Florida means "flowery." Those Spanish names never left the map. Now you can do something new with them: use ESTAR to say where each one is. Read these out loud:

  • Florida está en el sureste. — Florida is in the southeast.
  • California está en el oeste. — California is in the west.
  • Los Ángeles está en California.
  • El Paso está en Texas.

Every one uses está — the "he/she/it" form of ESTAR — because we're saying where, and "where" is always ESTAR.

🎮 PLAY · WHAT DOES THE PLACE NAME MEAN?

A quick refresher on the Spanish hiding in these US place names. Guess, then read the reveal — you can't lose.

✅ APPLY IT · SER OR ESTAR?
Now put it together. For each one, decide which verb the sentence needs.
"Miami _____ en Florida." (Miami is in Florida — a location.)
"Yo _____ de Colorado." (I am from Colorado — where I'm from.)
"Nosotros _____ contentos en la fiesta." (We are happy at the party — a feeling right now.)
★ WHY THIS MATTERS

The Spanish-speaking explorers and settlers who named these places were describing what they saw: red rivers, snowy peaks, mountain passes, fields of flowers. Every time you say Colorado está en el oeste, you're using Spanish to talk about a Spanish name — a small reminder that this language helped map the country you live in.
