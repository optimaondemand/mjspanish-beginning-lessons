👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
Every time you said soy de Florida, you were already using this week's verb — you just didn't know its name yet.

This week you meet SER, the Spanish verb for "to be." It is the most useful verb in the language: it's how you say who you are, what you are, and where you're from. And here's the friendly part — you've been borrowing one of its pieces for months.

🔄 FIRST · WHAT YOU ALREADY OWN

You have been saying short, ready-made Spanish "chunks" since the first weeks of this course. Read these out loud — you already know all three:

Me llamo… — My name is… Soy de… — I'm from… Tengo… años — I'm … years old

Look closely at the middle one: soy de Florida = "I am from Florida." That little word soy means "I am." It's one form of SER. This week you'll meet the rest of the family — and learn the pattern that connects them.

🎯 BY THE END OF THIS WEEK YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
explain what it means to "conjugate" a verb — using English "to be" as your guide.
2
use the subject pronouns (yo, tú, usted, él, ella…) — the little words for who does something.
3
say all five forms of SER and use them to tell people who and what you are.
