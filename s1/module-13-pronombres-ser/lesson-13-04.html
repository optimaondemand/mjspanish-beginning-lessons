LESSON 13.04 · SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD
Why "I am a doctor" was almost free
The words for who people are often look the same in both languages — here's why.
🌍 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
Words that look alike and mean the same in two languages are called cognates — and "identity" words are full of them.

Yesterday you learned doctor, profesor, estudiante, artista, actor and probably thought, "Wait, I already knew those." You did! English and Spanish both grew out of Latin, so thousands of words are near-twins — especially the words for jobs and roles. Read these Spanish words and notice the English hiding inside each one:

doctoractorprofesorartistaestudiantepresidente

Every one is a cognate — a free gift from the shared history of the two languages. Let's see how many you can decode.

🎮 PLAY · SPOT THE ENGLISH COUSIN

Take your best guess for each Spanish word, then read the reveal. You can't lose — every round is a word you now own in both languages.

📖 READ · A LITTLE PARAGRAPH YOU CAN ALREADY UNDERSTAND

Read this out loud. Between SER and the cognates, you can understand every word — try it before you check the notes:

¡Hola! Yo soy Ana. Soy estudiante. Mi mamá es doctora y mi papá es profesor. Nosotros somos de México.

Notes: Hi! I am Ana. I'm a student. My mom is a doctor and my dad is a teacher. We are from Mexico. — five SER forms and three cognates, and you read it cold.

★ WHY THIS MATTERS

The very first thing you learn to say in a new language is often "who I am." It turns out those exact words — doctor, artista, estudiante — are ones English already borrowed. Starting a language isn't starting from nothing. It's discovering how much you already share.
