LESSON 13.02 · THE IDEA BEHIND EVERY SPANISH VERB
How Conjugation Works
Learn this one idea and every Spanish verb for the rest of the year makes sense. Take it slowly.
🔥 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
A verb changes its shape depending on who is doing it. English does this too — you just never had to think about it.

Changing a verb's shape to match who's doing it is called conjugating it. It sounds like a big grammar word, but you already do it in English without noticing. Let's prove it.

📚 PART 1 · YOU ALREADY CONJUGATE IN ENGLISH

Take the English verb "to be." Watch what happens as you change who's doing it — say each line out loud:

🇬🇧 ENGLISH — the verb "to be"

  • I am
  • you are
  • he / she is
  • we are
  • they are

Notice: it's one verb, but the shape keeps changing — am, are, is, are, are. Nobody taught you a rule for that; you just know it. That is conjugation. Spanish does exactly the same thing — only it's more organized, so it's easier to learn on purpose.

👤 PART 2 · THE "WHO" WORDS (SUBJECT PRONOUNS)

Before we conjugate, we need the words for who. These are the subject pronouns — the Spanish for I, you, he, she, we, they:

SpanishMeans
yoI
túyou (a friend, someone your age — familiar)
ustedyou (polite / respectful — the safe default with adults and strangers)
él / ellahe / she
nosotros / nosotraswe (-os for a group with any males; -as for an all-female group)
ustedesyou all (more than one "you")
ellos / ellasthey (ellos = any group with males; ellas = all female)
💡 A gentle heads-up on "you": Spanish has more than one word for "you." Use tú with a friend and usted with an adult, a teacher, or anyone you want to show respect. In most of Latin America, usted is the safe, polite default — so when in doubt, use it. (You may also see vosotros for "you all" — that's used mainly in Spain. We'll stick with ustedes.)
🔊 PART 3 · SER, FORM BY FORM — ESCUCHA Y REPITE

Now put the "who" words together with SER (to be). Press ▶ on each row and say the form back out loud. There are only five forms to learn:

WhoSERMeaningListen
yosoyI am
túeresyou are (familiar)
usted / él / ellaesyou are (polite) / he is / she is
nosotros / nosotrassomoswe are
ustedes / ellos / ellassonyou all are / they are

Two things worth noticing. First, one form — es — covers "you (polite)," "he," and "she." That's a gift: three meanings, one word. Second, the form already tells you who: soy means "I am" all by itself, so Spanish speakers often drop the pronoun and just say soy de Florida. You don't have to say yo every time.

➡️ ONE IDEA TO CARRY OUT · WHAT'S NEXT

A verb changes shape to match who's doing it — and SER has just five shapes: soy, eres, es, somos, son. Say them once more before you close the page.
