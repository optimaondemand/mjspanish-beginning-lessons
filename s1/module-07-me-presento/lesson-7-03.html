Escucha y repite — the phrases that make it warm
New vocabulary, on the introduction recipe you already know.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A handful of friendly phrases turn a list of facts into a real, warm introduction.
You already have the five-step recipe. Today you learn the phrases that fill step 4 — and one question that lets you turn the introduction into a two-way conversation. Press ▶ to hear each one, then say it back out loud.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
say mucho gusto, encantado/a, and igualmente with clean Spanish vowels.
2
choose encantado or encantada depending on whether the speaker is male or female.
3
ask someone where they're from, politely (usted) or with a friend (tú).
🔊 PART 1 · ESCUCHA Y REPITE (LISTEN & REPEAT)

Mucho gusto.

Nice to meet you. (literally: "much pleasure")

Igualmente.

Likewise. / Same here. (a perfect reply to "mucho gusto")

El gusto es mío.

The pleasure is mine. (a warm, slightly more formal reply)

♂♀ PART 2 · ONE PHRASE THAT CHANGES WITH YOU

There's another way to say "nice to meet you": encantado ("delighted"). Here's the interesting part — the ending changes depending on who is speaking. Remember the -o/-a pattern from Module 2? It shows up here, with people, exactly the way it did with amigo / amiga:

♂ A boy or man says:

Encantado.
ends in -o — the speaker is male

♀ A girl or woman says:

Encantada.
ends in -a — the speaker is female
💡 You choose based on yourself, not the other person. A boy always says encantado; a girl always says encantada — no matter who they're meeting. (And if you'd rather keep it simple, mucho gusto never changes and works for everyone.)
🔊 PART 3 · ASK BACK: WHERE ARE YOU FROM?

You can already say Soy de… ("I'm from…"). Now flip it around and ask the other person. Just like greetings, there's a polite usted version and a friendly tú version:

¿De dónde eres?

Where are you from? — friendly (tú), for a classmate or someone your age

¿De dónde es usted?

Where are you from? — polite (usted), for an adult or someone you respect

🌐 In most of Latin America, usted is the safe, respectful default with adults you don't know. When in doubt, use usted — it's never rude.
