LESSON 7.04 · SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD
"Soy de…" — a whole story in two words
Where families come from is a big part of who they are — and it's all around you.
🌍 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
When someone says Soy de México or Soy de Puerto Rico, they're sharing a piece of their family's story — and millions of people in the United States can say exactly that.

More than 60 million people in the US have roots in the Spanish-speaking world — roughly one in every five people. They're your classmates, neighbors, teachers, and favorite athletes. The little phrase you just learned, soy de…, is how families all over the country name where they come from. Let's meet a few of those places.

🔍 EXPLORE · WHERE FAMILIES COME FROM

Tap each card to discover one place that many US Hispanic families call home. Read the Soy de… line out loud as you go.

📖 A SHORT READ · TWO INTRODUCTIONS

Read these two introductions out loud. You understand every word — and notice how soy de… quietly carries each person's story:

¡Hola! Me llamo Sofía. Soy de Miami, pero mi familia es de Cuba. Mucho gusto.

Buenos días. Me llamo Diego. Soy de Texas, y mi familia es de México. Encantado.

Did you catch pero ("but") and mi familia es de… ("my family is from…")? You don't need to produce those yet — but you understood them, because you already own the pieces around them.

✅ APPLY IT
Use what you just read and explored.
A classmate says: ¡Hola! Soy de Guatemala. What just happened?
Which heritage is the largest group among Hispanic families in the US?
Sofía wants to finish her introduction with "delighted." She should say…
★ WHY THIS MATTERS

When you learn to say soy de…, you're not just learning grammar — you're learning to hear the stories all around you. The next time a classmate introduces themselves, you'll understand a little more of who they are.
